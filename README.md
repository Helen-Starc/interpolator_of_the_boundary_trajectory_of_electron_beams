# interpolator_of_the_boundary_trajectory_of_electron_beams
Interpolator of the boundary trajectory of electron beams
The program has the following functionality:
1.	Solving a system of algebraic-differential equations using a numerical method.
2.	Modeling an electron beam with specified parameters such as accelerating voltage, beam current, working pressure, initial radius and convergence angle of the beam, initial and final points by the z-coordinate, and the number of calculation points.
3.	Visualization of modeling results on a graph showing the boundary trajectory of the electron beam.
4.	Selection of the best modeling result and clearing the graph window.
5.	Interpolation of the boundary trajectory of the electron beam using root-polynomial functions of the second, third, fourth, fifth, and sixth orders.
6.	Determination of the focus position of the electron beam, beam radius at the focus, current density, and beam power at the focus.
7.	Determination of the error in estimating the focus position and focal radius of the electron beam based on interpolation results.
8.	Displaying the results of interpolation on the graph.
9.	Processing and analysis of experimental research results, comparing the obtained results with the calculated parameters.
10.	Overlaying graphs for more effective comparison of results.
In general, the program allows modeling the behavior of an electron beam in a given system. It provides the ability to solve algebraic-differential equations, model beam trajectory, perform trajectory interpolation, and process and analyze experimental research results.
Specifically, the program can help determine the focus position of the electron beam, beam radius at the focus, current density, and beam power at the focus. It can also estimate the error in estimating the focus position and focal radius of the electron beam based on interpolation results.
Depending on the input data and parameters, the program allows calculating and visualizing various quantities and characteristics of the electron beam. This can be useful in the design and analysis of systems that utilize electron beams, such as electron microscopes, particle accelerators, electron ballistic systems, and other domains where the behavior of charged particle beams is important.
